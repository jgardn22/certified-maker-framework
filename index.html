Start here, before anyone builds anything

One Pledge. Every employee.

Before we talk about makers, let's talk about everyone. Shadow AI is the new shadow IT. Anyone can paste customer data into a chatbot, whether or not they ever build a single app or agent. So the first move is not a training path. It is an attestation.

The Responsible AI Pledge
"I commit to using AI responsibly: respecting company data policies, keeping sensitive information out of unapproved tools, and following the guidelines provided."
Signed by every employee, whether or not they intend to touch a Microsoft AI tool

You do not earn your way into the Pledge. You start there. It is Level Zero, and everyone is already on it. The bare minimum that comes with it is a short AI literacy path, not a course catalog.

The one idea that changes everything

Guardrails over gates.

Here is the heresy. You believe more governance means slower makers. The opposite is true, and the fastest maker programs on earth are also the most governed.

A gate asks

"Why should I let you?"

Nothing moves until someone approves it. The CoE becomes the bottleneck.

A guardrail asks

"How do I help you do this safely?"

Yes by default. The environment itself nudges makers to build safely.

Same platform. Opposite posture. And trust runs both ways: the CoE extends trust to the maker by saying yes safely, and the maker trusts the governance process in return. That is the deal. Everything else in this framework is just that one shift, applied again and again.

The one rule that keeps this humane

The bar is competency, not mastery.

Here is the discipline that keeps every checkpoint honest. At each rung, the question is never "are you an expert." It is "can you build one real thing, safely, and publish it once."

Recruit means "know everything about agents"→Ground one agent in one approved source and publish it
Bronze means "master the platform"→Build one app, one flow, and share it with your team

Trust is earned in the smallest verifiable increment, then the ladder hands you the next one. Set the bar at mastery and nobody climbs. Set it at competency and everybody moves.

Building your program

Five decisions that turn this into your program.

1 · Design your ladder

What each level requires, and what it unlocks, for every track.

2 · Design your access architecture

Which environment a maker builds in at each level, and the gates before Novice even begins.

3 · Commit to service levels

Turn governance from a black box into a service with published SLAs.

4 · Define the certification lifecycle

Treat certification as a status you can suspend or revoke, not a one time milestone.

5 · Add agent-specific governance

Copilot Studio's risk surface, and its shared credit pool, need their own controls.

These five decisions are the work of standing up a CoE. Everything before this was concept. Everything from here is construction.

Building your program, decision 1

Design your ladder.

The method is one question, asked four times: what is the smallest privilege we can safely grant, and what does someone have to prove before we grant more? That single question produces the entire ladder.

Building your program, decision 2

Govern by zone, not by app.

You do not govern by policing every build. You route each app, flow, and agent into one of three risk zones, and the zone decides the environment, the controls, and who owns it. This is Microsoft's recommended risk-based model: match governance to risk instead of treating everything the same.

🟢 Green · Maker-Led Innovation
Typical work
Personal apps, individual Copilot Studio agents, proofs of concept, team helpers
Governance

Goal

🟡 Yellow · Fusion Development

Governance

Owner
Business + IT partnership, the Fusion Team
Goal
Agility with reliability and governance
🔴 Red · Enterprise / Pro Dev
Typical work
Mission-critical, regulated, or customer-facing solutions and high-scale integrations
Governance
Formal SDLC, Dev/Test/Prod, source control, security reviews, production monitoring, change management
Owner
IT / Engineering / Pro Dev teams
Goal
Reliability, compliance, security, supportability

Route the work first, then assign the people, environments, security, ALM, and support. A solution does not start in Red because it uses Power Platform. It starts in the zone that matches its risk, and moves up only when it must.

Before anyone enters Green, every maker clears three gates: manager sign-off, security training, and a data-classification quiz at 100 percent.

Building your program, decision 4

Certification is a status, not a trophy.

Most organizations treat a certification like a diploma on the wall, earned once and kept forever. Wrong mental model. It grants real platform access, so it has to behave like an access control: auditable, reversible, time bound. Stop thinking diploma. Start thinking door, a door you can close.

StatusMeaningWhat triggers it
ActiveFull privileges of the certified tierSuccessful promotion review
SuspendedPrivileges paused pending remediationA DLP violation or failed check on a production solution
RevokedPrivileges removed; re-application requiredRepeated violations or deliberate policy bypass
Lapsed / ReinstatedPaused for an overdue step, then restoredA required artifact is overdue, then verified

Building your program, decision 5

Add agent-specific governance.

The first four decisions work for every track. This is the deliberate exception. An agent does not fail with a broken formula you can see. It fails by hallucinating or oversharing, quietly and plausibly. That one difference earns agents a single extra layer, four controls that answer four questions.

Knowledge · what it knows

One approved list of sources an agent may ground on, reviewed quarterly for drift.

Reach · where it goes

Test canvas for Bronze, broader internal for Silver, public web with elevated sign-off for Gold.

Safety · is it sound

Grounding documented, adversarial prompts tested, fallback defined, human handoff configured.

Cost · what it burns

Assign a capacity tier at plan time and monitor burn rate. The Copilot Credit pool is shared and finite.

Knowledge, Reach, Safety, Cost. If an agent clears all four, it has earned its rung.

Bringing makers along

What a maker adds on top of the Pledge.

Every employee already signed the Responsible AI Pledge. This is the thin layer of build specific commitments that unlocks the moment Maria decides to make something. Nothing here is designed to slow her down. Each step grants more freedom, and there is no clock.

  • 1Use only approved connectors for business data.
  • 2Keep solutions in the environment that matches your level.
  • 3Accept that your solutions may be reviewed for quality and compliance.

A license is not enablement. Free training supplements exist for every track, all optional. The Pledge is the requirement. The training is the on-ramp.

Keeping it human

Keep the framework enabling, not stifling.

The mechanics can be perfect and the program can still fail on tone. If Maria experiences this as bureaucracy, she quietly goes back to building around you, the exact problem we opened with.

🧭

Clear path & communication

Give makers a roadmap from day one. Knowing the rules up front dispels confusion.

🏅

Recognition & community

Badges for Bronze, Silver, Gold, showcases, and a community of practice moderated by champions.

⚖

Fairness & flexibility

Apply criteria equally, keep promotions merit based, and listen when the process feels too slow.

Knowing it is working

The six signals of a healthy program.

A healthy program is rising maker and agent count with flat or falling risk indicators. Rising count with rising incidents is the warning sign that a guardrail quietly became a gate, or the reverse.
